2. **Unemployment-Insurance Trust Fund**: As employment decreases, fewer workers are paying into unemployment insurance, reducing the trust fund's reserves. With fewer jobs, more individuals seek unemployment benefits, further depleting the fund.

3. **Withholding Receipts**: Reduced employment also means lower withholding receipts for states, as fewer employees are contributing to state taxes through payroll deductions. This decrease in receipts can lead to budget shortfalls.

4. **General-Obligation Municipal Bonds**: The pressure on general-obligation municipal bonds arises from reduced tax receipts and increased unemployment, which can lead to decreased creditworthiness and higher interest rates for borrowing municipalities.

5. **AI-Driven Productivity Gains**: Simultaneously, AI-driven productivity gains suppress wage growth. This suppression affects projected future tax receipts, as lower wages mean less income tax collected.

   - **Year 2**: Continued contraction and unemployment lead to further decreases in receipts and trust fund reserves.
   - **Year 3**: By this point, the compounding effect is significant, with substantial depletion of unemployment-insurance trust funds and reduced withholding receipts.
