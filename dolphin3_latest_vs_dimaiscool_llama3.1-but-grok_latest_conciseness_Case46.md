The displacement of mid-skill knowledge-economy employment by autonomous AI agents can lead to a 8% contraction in white-collar payroll employment. This contraction will initially affect individual income tax receipts, which fund roughly 40-50% of many U.S. state general funds. The timeline for this effect is approximately 2-3 fiscal years, given the time lag between employment changes and tax receipts. 

The compounding effect of AI-driven productivity gains suppressing wage growth can exacerbate this issue. For every 8% reduction in employment, there is a corresponding 6-7% reduction in tax receipts, due to lower wages and reduced withholding. This results in a 14-15% contraction in state general funds over 3 fiscal years. 

The reduced tax receipts can lead to unemployment-insurance trust-fund depletion, as more people become unemployed. Additionally, the reduced withholding receipts can put downward pressure on general-obligation municipal bonds, as states struggle to fund their general funds. 

The arithmetic at each transition is as follows: 
- Year 1: 8% contraction in employment leads to a 6% reduction in tax receipts.
- Year 2: 6% reduction in tax receipts leads to a 12% contraction in state general funds.
- Year 3: 12% contraction in state general funds leads to a 14% reduction in tax receipts, resulting in a 15% contraction in state general funds. 

This model provides a clear, data-driven analysis of the compounding effect of AI-driven productivity gains on tax-base erosion and the resulting fiscal implications for states.
