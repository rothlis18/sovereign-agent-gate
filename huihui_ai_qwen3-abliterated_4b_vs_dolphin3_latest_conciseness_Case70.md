**Systems-Engineering Analysis: Tax-Base Erosion from AI-Driven Employment Displacement**  

**1. Baseline Assumptions**  
- **Current State Revenue**: 40–50% of state general funds derive from individual income tax (IIT), with 20–25% from unemployment insurance (UI) trust funds.  
- **Payroll Employment**: 8% contraction in white-collar (mid-skill) employment (e.g., engineers, analysts, consultants).  
- **Productivity Gains**: AI reduces labor costs by 15–20% per displaced worker, but compresses wage growth by 3–5% annually.  
- **UI Trust Fund**: 1.5–2.0% of state GDP, funded by 0.4–0.6% of payroll taxes.  
- **Municipal Bonds**: 40–50% of municipal debt is general-obligation (GO) bonds, rated by agencies like S&P, Moody’s, or Fitch.

- **Wage Growth Compression**: 3% annual decline in wage growth reduces future tax receipts by 0.1–0.2% per year. Over 5 years, this reduces projected IIT revenue by 1.2–1.5% of total state funds.  
- **UI Trust Fund Depletion**: At 1.2M/year depletion, with 2024–2028 trust fund balance of $27M–$34M, depletion occurs in 2.2–2.8 years.  
- **Municipal Bond Ratings**: GO bonds rated on 40–50% of municipal debt. With 8% payroll decline, state revenue drops 1.2–1.5M/year, reducing bond rating capacity by 0.3–0.5% annually. S&P/Moody’s downgrade triggers 0.5–1.0% credit spread increase.
