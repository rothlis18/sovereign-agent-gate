### Systems-Engineering Analysis of Tax-Base Erosion Due to AI-Driven Employment Displacement

#### Overview

The displacement of mid-skill knowledge-economy employment by autonomous AI agents can significantly impact municipal and state tax bases. This analysis explores the fiscal implications over a fiscal-year timeline, focusing on the erosion of individual income tax receipts, unemployment-insurance trust-fund depletion, and the pressure on general-obligation municipal bonds.

#### Fiscal-Year Timeline Analysis

1. **Initial Employment Contraction (Year 0-1)**
   - **8% Contraction in White-Collar Payroll Employment**: 
     - Immediate reduction in individual income tax receipts due to decreased employment.
     - Assuming a linear relationship, a 8% reduction in employment translates to an approximate 8% reduction in income tax receipts from this sector.
     - **Impact on State General Funds**: If individual income taxes fund 40-50% of state general funds, an 8% reduction in this revenue stream could lead to a 3.2-4% shortfall in total state funding.

2. **Unemployment-Insurance Trust-Fund Depletion (Year 1-2)**
   - **Increased Unemployment Claims**: 
     - As displaced workers file for unemployment benefits, the trust fund experiences increased outflows.
     - Assuming a baseline unemployment rate of 5%, an 8% increase in unemployment could lead to a proportional increase in claims.
     - **Trust-Fund Depletion**: If the trust fund is initially balanced, increased claims without corresponding increases in contributions could deplete reserves within 1-2 years, depending on the fund's initial surplus and the duration of unemployment benefits.

3. **Reduced Withholding Receipts (Year 2-3)**
   - **Wage Suppression and Reduced Tax Withholding**:
     - AI-driven productivity gains suppress wage growth, further reducing tax withholding.
     - If wage growth is suppressed by 2% annually, this could lead to a compounded reduction in tax receipts over time.
     - **Compounding Effect**: Over three years, a 2% annual suppression could result in a cumulative reduction of approximately 6% in tax receipts from wage growth alone.

4. **Downgrade Pressure on General-Obligation Municipal Bonds (Year 3-4)**
   - **Credit Rating Agencies' Response**:
     - Reduced tax receipts and depleted trust funds increase fiscal stress on municipalities.
     - Credit rating agencies may downgrade bonds due to perceived increased risk of default.
     - **Impact on Borrowing Costs**: Downgrades typically lead to higher interest rates on new debt, increasing the cost of borrowing for municipalities.

#### Mechanism and Arithmetic

- **Year 0-1**: 
  - Initial 8% employment contraction leads to a 3.2-4% reduction in state general funds.
  
- **Year 1-2**: 
  - Increased unemployment claims deplete trust funds, potentially leading to a need for state intervention or increased borrowing.

- **Year 2-3**: 
  - 2% annual wage suppression results in a 6% cumulative reduction in tax receipts over three years.

- **Year 3-4**: 
  - Fiscal stress leads to bond downgrades, increasing borrowing costs and further straining municipal budgets.

#### Conclusion

The displacement of mid-skill knowledge-economy employment by AI agents can lead to significant fiscal challenges for municipalities and states. The erosion of tax bases, depletion of unemployment-insurance trust funds, and increased borrowing costs due to bond downgrades create a compounding effect that exacerbates fiscal stress over time. Addressing these challenges requires proactive policy measures to mitigate the impact on employment and tax revenues.
